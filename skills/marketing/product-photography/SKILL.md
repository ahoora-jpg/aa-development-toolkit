# Product Photography

## Purpose

How to shoot and present product images for an e-commerce store — critical for a home appliance retailer, since shoppers cannot touch the product and images carry the entire purchase decision. Directly supports the product-page conversion elements in `skills/marketing/conversion-rate-optimization/`.

### Amazon Product Image Requirements

Source:
https://www.amazon.com (seller/image guideline documentation)

Notes: Amazon's main-image rules (pure white background, product filling ~85% of frame, no added text/graphics) have become a de facto industry baseline that most marketplaces and shoppers now expect, even outside Amazon itself.

## Core Concepts To Apply

- **Five to eight images per product minimum**: cover a clean hero shot, multiple angles, close-up detail shots (materials, controls, finish), a scale reference, and at least one lifestyle/in-context shot — fewer images measurably hurts conversion for considered purchases like appliances.
- **Pure white background for the primary image**: the main product image should have the product filling roughly 80–90% of the frame on a clean white or near-white background — this is now a near-universal shopper expectation, not just a marketplace rule, and keeps a product grid looking clean and professional.
- **Lifestyle images for context, not as the main image**: a secondary shot showing the appliance in a real kitchen/home setting helps a buyer picture it in their own space — reserve this for supporting images, since the primary marketplace-style image should stay on clean white.
- **Consistency across the whole catalog**: same lighting setup, same crop ratio, same background treatment, same white balance across every product — an inconsistent mix of photo styles across a catalog reads as unprofessional even if individual photos are good.
- **You don't need professional studio equipment to start**: a modern smartphone, a basic light kit or even natural window light, a tripod, and a plain white backdrop can produce acceptable results for most product categories — professional equipment improves quality but isn't a hard requirement to begin.
- **Consider 360° or multi-angle views for higher-consideration items**: for larger appliances where customers want to inspect multiple sides/details before buying, an interactive 360° spin or an expanded angle set can measurably reduce hesitation and returns — treat this as a worthwhile investment for higher-price items, not required for every product.
- **Show scale and real dimensions**: appliances have real-world size implications for a customer's kitchen or home — including a clear size/dimension reference in at least one image reduces the most common source of buyer uncertainty and post-purchase returns.

## When To Use

Establish the photography style (background, lighting, angle set, crop ratio) once, as a documented standard, before shooting the first product catalog — retrofitting consistency onto hundreds of already-shot product photos is far more expensive than defining the standard up front.
